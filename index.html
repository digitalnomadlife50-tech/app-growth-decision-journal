WIKIPEDIA APPS / APP GROWTH

Continue reading

A product decision journal
ArticleDiscussionReadView source

Help people pick up where curiosity left off.

This journal documents the reasoning behind a six-week proposal for a “Continue reading” experience in the Wikipedia apps. It describes the evidence, assumptions, trade-offs, and decisions that shaped the proposed first release.

Scope of this document: The assignment scenario and its numbers are fictional. Facts below come from the supplied brief. Interpretations, hypotheses, and proposed decisions are identified as such. This is a companion to the requested interview deliverables, not a replacement for them. The requested deliverables retain the labels used in the brief: A. Scope and measurement plan; B. Phabricator epic and two user story tasks; C. Slack update to the Sr. Director; and D. Public reply to the volunteer editor.

The assignment [edit]

The quarterly goal is to increase the share of new app installs that return within seven days. Leadership is interested in a “Continue reading” feature, with a first version expected within six weeks.

The PM owns scope, delivery, and updates to the Senior Director while the manager is away. The exercise asks for a scope and measurement plan, a Phabricator epic and two complete stories, a leadership update, and a public reply to a volunteer editor.

Starting evidence [edit]

The supplied 30-day snapshot:

MetricAndroidiOS
New installs returning within 7 days22%28%
Sessions ending partway through an article41%38%
Push notification opt-in rate61%34%

Support separately reports that the app loses a reader’s place when backgrounded. An app review complains about notification volume. A volunteer editor asks whether the app will store what people read and where that data goes.

The analyst warns that the Android drop in new installs last month is partly due to a tracking change. Direct month-over-month comparison is therefore not appropriate.

The product question [edit]

Is the problem that readers need another reason to open Wikipedia, or that the app makes it unnecessarily difficult to continue something they already started?

We chose to test continuity before adding another engagement mechanic.

Reader journey and target segment [edit]

The proposed audience is not every new reader, and it is not everyone whose session ends mid-article. It is new app readers who interrupt an article and would benefit from being able to return to it. The brief does not tell us how many readers fit that description or how many want to resume. That is the central user hypothesis to test, not a fact to assume.

Journey stageReader's likely jobPotential friction
DiscoverFind an answer or follow a questionNot the focus of this intervention
Open and readUnderstand an article at their own paceArticle experience must remain useful and stable
InterruptLeave temporarily, switch tasks, or stopApp may lose the current position when backgrounded
ReturnContinue the earlier reading or find it againReader may need to relocate the article and reconstruct where they were
Resume or move onContinue, dismiss, or finishFeature must preserve reader choice and make dismissal simple

The intervention targets the handoff between interruption and return. It does not try to solve discovery, create a reason to read longer, or treat leaving as a negative action. This is a narrower and more testable problem than “increase engagement.”

Alternatives considered [edit]

Before selecting the first release, we compared three distinct approaches against the quarterly outcome, evidence in the brief, delivery constraints, and user trust.

ApproachPotential valueWhy it was not the first-release choice
1. Restore the last position automaticallyFixes the reported loss of place when a reader backgrounds an articleUseful for immediate recovery, but alone it does not make an older interrupted article easy to find after the reader has left or opened other content
2. Surface interrupted articles on HomeCreates a visible return path without requiring a notification or accountNeeds reliable saved position and a way to restore it. A list without persistence would only solve part of the problem
3. Send a reminder notificationCould bring a reader back after they leave the appIntent to return is unknown, notification fatigue is already reported, and Android limits non-essential notifications to one per user per week

Decision: combine local position persistence with a small Home entry point. The two parts form one complete return path: remember where the reader stopped, then make that article findable when they come back. This is more directly connected to the observed friction than a reminder, fits the six-week constraint better than sync, and preserves the reader's choice about whether to resume.

The alternatives are product approaches, not experimentally validated solutions. The brief provides no comparative user research or feature-effect data. The choice is a reasoned starting point to test.

Ecosystem and leverage [edit]

The feature sits inside a wider system. The decision should create value for readers without creating hidden costs for the people who maintain, support, or trust Wikipedia.

ParticipantWhat matters to themProduct implication
ReadersUseful continuity, control, and a clear way to move onResume, dismiss, and clear history. Do not infer intent from an interruption
Volunteer editors and the wider communityTrust that reading behavior is not quietly turned into a personal profileMake data boundaries understandable and verify the actual storage and telemetry paths
SupportFewer reports of lost reading position and confusing behaviorTest backgrounding, navigation, restoration, and failure states
Engineering and DesignA buildable, accessible scope that fits platform realitiesUse local persistence, shared behavior requirements, explicit fallbacks, and platform-specific implementation
MarketingA timely campaign with a compelling storyOffer a discovery-led campaign using existing sharing rather than adding streak mechanics for the first release
App GrowthMore new readers returning within seven daysTest the outcome with a holdout and keep platform results separate

The highest-leverage point the team can influence within six weeks is the return path inside the app. It is grounded in a support-reported usability issue, can be built without account infrastructure, and can be tested against the stated team outcome. This does not establish that continuity is the largest possible growth opportunity across Wikipedia. It establishes that it is a plausible, actionable opportunity within this brief.

The trade-offs [edit]

1. Incomplete does not mean unfinished

A session ending partway through an article is an observable event, not proof that the reader wanted to return. Some people got what they needed. Others were interrupted. The feature should help the second group without treating every exit as a failure.

Product implication

Use interruption as an eligibility signal, not as evidence of intent or a reason to send a prompt.

2. Retention versus trust

Reading history can make continuity useful, but it can also feel like surveillance. The volunteer editor’s question makes this a product requirement, not a footer disclaimer.

Product implication

Propose local-only storage, no account requirement, no article-level analytics, and visible controls to dismiss or clear. Verify the full data path before release.

3. Cross-device convenience versus six-week feasibility

iOS local persistence is described as easy. Sync requires account work and at least a quarter. A cross-device experience may be more complete, but it does not fit the stated release window.

Product implication

Start local-first. Treat sync as a later hypothesis to revisit if readers value continuity beyond one device.

4. More prompts versus notification fatigue

Push opt-in is higher on Android than iOS, but that does not establish that notifications are the right intervention. The app review explicitly complains about too many notifications, and Android has a one-per-week cap for non-essential notifications.

Product implication

Use an in-product return path first. Do not spend the notification budget to solve a problem that may be addressed in the app.

5. Marketing visibility versus product focus

A reading-streak card could be campaign-friendly, but it introduces a social engagement mechanic and may increase pressure to collect or expose reading behavior.

Product implication

Decline it for the first release and offer a discovery-led campaign using existing sharing capabilities.

The first-release decision [edit]

Save interrupted reading positions on-device. Surface up to three recent articles on Home. Let readers resume, dismiss, or clear their history.

Included in the first releaseDeferred
  • Local position persistence
  • Continue reading on Home
  • Resume at saved position
  • Dismiss one article
  • Clear all reading history
  • Remove completed articles
  • Privacy-safe diagnostic events
  • Cross-device sync
  • Push notifications
  • Reading streaks or social cards
  • Personalized recommendations
  • AI summaries or extra prompts

Help people pick up their curiosity where they left it. Do not turn curiosity into a retention mechanic.

How we would know [edit]

Primary outcome: the existing share of new app installs that return within seven days. Preserve the established event and denominator definition. Randomize eligible new installs 50/50 to treatment and holdout, analyze intention-to-treat, and report Android and iOS separately.

Proposed decision threshold: at least +2 percentage points absolute lift versus holdout on each platform. This is a decision threshold for the exercise, not a forecast. The analyst validates power, minimum detectable effect, sample size, and duration.

Guardrails: app stability, article-load success, restoration success, uninstalls, app reviews, support contacts, and privacy complaints. Feature adoption is diagnostic, not the outcome.

Measurement caution: Do not compare Android directly with the previous month. The brief says the Android drop is partly explained by a tracking change. If the six-week point arrives before the experiment is adequately powered, report the result as inconclusive and continue measurement.

What remains open [edit]

The proposal is specific about user-visible behavior and provisional about implementation details that belong with the team. These are first validation points after kickoff, not reasons to hold the interview submission.

What changed in the work

  1. We kept the original outcome and did not replace the assignment metric with a new North Star.
  2. We sharpened the target from “people with incomplete sessions” to readers who would benefit from resuming, while acknowledging that intent is unknown.
  3. We mapped the return journey and identified the precise friction point.
  4. We made the solution choice comparative, not feature-led.
  5. We mapped stakeholder interests and tied the decision to the team's actual leverage and six-week horizon.
  6. We kept the core submission concise and placed the fuller reasoning here, where it can be examined without burdening the requested deliverables.

Working method [edit]

  1. Start with the stated outcome, not the feature request.
  2. Map the reader journey before choosing the intervention.
  3. Separate supplied evidence from interpretation, assumptions, and hypotheses.
  4. Compare distinct approaches, then make a clear choice against impact, feasibility, and trust.
  5. Use constraints to narrow the first release, not to lower the quality bar.
  6. Make privacy and reader control part of the experience definition.
  7. Specify behavior clearly, then leave platform implementation to Engineering.
  8. Define what result would change the next decision.
